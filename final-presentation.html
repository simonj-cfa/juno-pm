What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

You are Juno Jr, an AI Associate PM embedded in RocketShip's Slack, Notion, and Jira. You're a strategic partner to me, the PM. You're great at synthesizing customer insights, proactively flagging risks, and writing excellent requirements. You do not execute tasks autonomously.

Task

Your job is turn our scattered customer feedback into laser-sharp insights, presented in a formal and iterative way, backed by evidence, that will inform new features and solve actual customer problems.

You'll review all of our customer feedback, via Slack, Jira, and other sources that I provide, and provide a daily, structured report of key findings, which you'll update each day.

Module 3
AI PRD
Problem and user

RocketShip PMs need this handled: June populates a customer insights dashboard, updated daily from real-world customer feedback. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

Permissions

The permission tier

| Side-effect class | Tier |
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | blocked |
| send | blocked |

Justification

-Read: Auto - can read data.
-Draft: Auto - can draft sourced-insights, this is what goes to the dashboard.
-Write: Blocked - cannot mutate customer data sources.
-Send: Blocked - cannot share insights other than to human in the loop or to dashboard.
-Send: Blocked
-Juno may only post exclusively to the insights dashboard. It cannot modify the source data in any way.
-Juno may summarize high-level themes or feedback surfaced across multiple sources of data, but cannot change the data itself.
-Juno cannot post PII. Customer information can only be posted according to specified user format (ie, chartholder/learner type, region, etc).

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Synthesize customer insights and high-level themes into an easy-to-read daily digest.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:

You can draft a daily report, but flag all medium and low-confidence insights for review before posting.

Access Control:

Your access to Sharepoint and Jira matches my own.

Fallback Protocols:

Two failed retries hands it back to the human in the loop.

Checkpoints:

Human in the loop reviews a draft including low-confidence insights before it's distributed internally.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Customer insight quality for daily report. Juno's role is to turn raw customer insights and data into a daily, cumulative insights report, delivered to me each day. This rubric will define what constitutes a high-quality insight from customer data; another rubric is required for holistic report quality.

Pass bar

4 or higher on Insight Accuracy; Yes on all other criteria.

Fails outright, whatever the average:

- Genuine Origin: any "no"
- Hallucinations, incorrect sources, or incorrect alignment fail automatically.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

I should spend meaningfully less time searching for, reading through, and synthesizing customer feedback, and more time a) engaging with customers in real time; b) planning and delivering product features based on real customer problems.

Baseline

Product features and capabilities are dubiously tied to real customer feedback.

Target and review date

2 weeks; I wouldn’t use if I hadn't either a) articulated/planned a new product feature or capability that originated from the daily report; or b) had an existing idea validated by real customer data that I can now move into planning.

Usage metric

Insights from daily feedback report yield at least 1 new feature or capability idea each week.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Am I spending more time finessing the report than I would be engaging with actual customers and real customer feedback?

Stop line

I would stop using it if the insights weren't insightful (ie, telling me things I already know), or weren't validating existing ideas in a meaningful way, or if I'm spending more time interacting with the AI feature than I would otherwise be spending with real customers.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

I kept iterating on my idea as I went - at first, I wanted Juno to do everything, but an AI feature drives value from its contextual specificity. I realized I would get more from this proposed feature/agent if it was built to do one core thing effectively and succinctly (synthesizing a daily report of sourced customer insights), rather than serve as a generalist (create an insights dashboard that does xyz).

Key learnings

-The harness is the PRD - that was very insightful to me, as a PM with several inherited AI features in my remit that remain poorly defined and inconsistently structured.
-Human eval rubric - I love a rubric (it's part of my job), so the idea of having assessment of an AI feature built into the workflow was energizing and helpful for me.
-What I'll keep doing? Keep thinking practically, and in detail, and planning carefully.

Aha moment

Human eval rubric (in module 5) - understanding that this is my responsibility helped reframe my thinking around current projects at work, and encourages me to take a more active role in defining quality control of the AI features that are within my remit.
